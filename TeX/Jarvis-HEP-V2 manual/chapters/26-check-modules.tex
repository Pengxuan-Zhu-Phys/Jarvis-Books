\chapter{The check\_modules Smoke Path}
\label{ch:check-modules}

\newthought{Test the workflow before you buy the scan.} The smoke path runs your full
calculator/opera/likelihood chain on a handful of points --- fixed from a \textsc{csv},
or drawn fresh from your real \yamlkey{Sampling.Method} when no \textsc{csv} is
configured --- with the per-sample command output mirrored to your terminal, so you can
validate a card in minutes instead of discovering a broken \yamlkey{entry} path an hour
into a real scan.

\section{Invocation}
\label{sec:check-invocation}

\begin{terminal}
\begin{jcmd}
Jarvis check task.yaml
\end{jcmd}
\end{terminal}

\textbf{The command is the whole switch.} There is no key in the task card that turns
this mode on, and nothing to add or take out again before a production run: the card you
smoke-test is byte-for-byte the card you later hand to \cli{Jarvis run}
(\S\ref{sec:cli-shape}). What the card \emph{may} carry is a budget --- how many points,
which \textsc{csv}, how long to wait --- and that lives under \yamlkey{EnvReqs.V2}, next
to every other runtime setting.

\section{Where the points come from}
\label{sec:check-points-source}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A \textsc{csv}, if one exists.} \yamlkey{EnvReqs.V2.check\_modules.data}
        names it, defaulting to
        \marker{\&J/}\file{data/\allowbreak{}check\_modules\_points.csv}. This is the
        only place in the card a check \textsc{csv} can be named --- and because
        \yamlkey{EnvReqs.V2} is what a shared defaults file merges
        (Section~\ref{sec:check-default-deps}), a whole project can point at one set of
        smoke points without repeating it on every card.
  \item \textbf{Otherwise, points drawn from your real sampler.} When that path names
        no file that actually exists, \Jtwo\ falls back to drawing
        \yamlkey{EnvReqs.V2.check\_modules.n\_samples} points (default \code{10}) from
        your real \yamlkey{Sampling.Method} --- the same sampler your production scan
        would use, including \sampler{Dynesty} and \sampler{MultiNest}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{yamlwin}
EnvReqs:
  V2:
    check_modules:
      data: "&J/data/check_points.csv"   # optional override
      n_samples: 10                      # sampler-fallback point count
      timeout_sec: 120                   # deadline for smoke samples to archive
\end{yamlwin}

\yamlkey{timeout\_sec} is a deadline, not a fixed wait --- a card whose points finish
sooner exits sooner. \cli{Jarvis check --timeout} overrides it for a single invocation
(\S\ref{sec:cli-check}).

Both routes end up at the same place: a small batch of \code{Sample}s runs through the
identical execution plan a real scan uses, and the console shows everything.

\begin{jnote}
\textbf{A \textsc{csv}'s columns are \emph{u-space} coordinates, one per declared
variable, in the order of \yamlkey{Sampling.Variables}} --- not physical parameter
values. This is the same unit-cube language every sampler proposes in
(Section~\ref{sec:uspace}); the Worker maps it to physical values exactly as it would
for any other Sample. Write \code{0.5} to mean the middle of a variable's declared
range, whatever that range is in physical units.
\end{jnote}

\begin{jwarn}
\textbf{Your card is validated in full, \textsc{csv} or not.}
\yamlkey{Sampling.Method} and \yamlkey{Sampling.Variables} are required of every card
(Chapter~\ref{ch:sampling-block}), and \cli{check} runs the same gate \cli{run} does ---
\yamlkey{Bounds}, distributions, everything in
Chapters~\ref{ch:multinest}--\ref{ch:dynesty} included --- \emph{even when} a
\textsc{csv} supplies the points and the sampler is never asked to propose anything. A
card cannot be smoke-tested past a broken \yamlkey{Method}; fix it first
(Section~\ref{sec:nested-validation}).
\end{jwarn}

Everything else in the card --- \yamlkey{Calculators}, \yamlkey{Operas},
\yamlkey{Likelihood}, \yamlkey{LibDeps} --- is exercised exactly as in a real scan:
same Workers, same execution plan, same archive path.

\section{What you see}
\label{sec:check-output}

For each point, the console shows what a production scan writes only to the Sample's
private log: the calculator banner, the resolved command line, the raw
stdout/stderr of every external command, and the captured observables --- ending in the
usual archive and a normal \file{DATABASE/} record. The mirroring uses the same
rendered text as the file log, so what you debug is what production records.

\section{The habit}
\label{sec:check-habit}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Keep \file{data/check\_modules\_points.csv} in the project: two or three points
        whose expected observables you know --- a published benchmark, a point you
        computed by hand, given as u-space coordinates for your declared
        \yamlkey{Variables}. That is the default path, so a project that uses this name
        needs no \yamlkey{check\_modules} block at all.
  \item Run \cli{Jarvis check} after \emph{any} edit to \yamlkey{execution} blocks,
        \yamlkey{Dump} variables, or \yamlkey{entry} paths.
  \item Compare the captured observables against the known values before launching the
        scan.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{jtip}
The check points double as an integration test for tool upgrades: after rebuilding an
external calculator, one \cli{Jarvis check} against known-good numbers tells you
whether physics changed before any scan does.
\end{jtip}

\begin{jtip}
No fixed points yet and just want a fast smoke of a \sampler{Dynesty} or
\sampler{MultiNest} card? Skip the \textsc{csv} entirely --- \cli{Jarvis check} draws
\code{n\_samples} real points from your \yamlkey{Bounds}/\yamlkey{Variables} and runs
them through the full pipeline, no separate fixture to maintain.
\end{jtip}
